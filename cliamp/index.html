cliamp

A personal OAuth client · not a public service

This page exists because Google’s OAuth consent screen asks every application to name a home page. So here is the honest description of what sits behind it.

cliamp is an open-source terminal music player — “Winamp for the shell” — written by a third-party author and released under the MIT License. Big Pic Solutions did not write it and does not maintain it.

What this OAuth client does

Google Cloud project cliamp-507800 holds a single Desktop OAuth client. It lets one person’s installation of cliamp, running on one computer, list that person’s own YouTube Music playlists and liked songs so they can be played back.

It requests one read-only scope, youtube.readonly. It cannot post, delete, edit, subscribe, or change anything on the account. It has no server, no database, and no users other than its operator.

Why it needed a website

An OAuth app left in Google’s “Testing” status is issued sign-in credentials that expire every seven days. Moving it to production stops the weekly expiry — and Google requires a home page, a privacy policy, and terms of service before it will allow that. These three pages are those documents. That is their entire purpose.

The documents

  • Privacy Policy — what is read, where it goes, how to revoke it.
  • Terms of Service — scope, warranty, liability.
